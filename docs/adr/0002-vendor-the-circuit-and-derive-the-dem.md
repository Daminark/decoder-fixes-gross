# 2. Vendor the syndrome-extraction circuit and derive the detector error model

Date: 2026-10-23

## Status

Accepted

## Context

The comparison runs on the circuit-level detector error model of the \[[144,12,12]\] gross code: 12 rounds of Z memory, Z detectors only, generated by the circuit builder of Gong et al. (MIT licence) at physical error rate 1e-3.
The published trapping-set lists (Pacenti, Raveendran and Vasić, arXiv:2608.11516) index the columns of this model, in order of first appearance in the flattened Stim detector error model after identical mechanisms are merged.

Two parts of the method need the model at other error rates.
The floor estimate of that paper weights each fault support by its probability at the physical error rate p, for p from 1e-4 to 1e-3, and the probability of each mechanism is read from the circuit at p.
The Monte Carlo check samples at p from 2e-3 to 4e-3 while the decoders keep the priors at 1e-3.
A detector error model stored at 1e-3 alone would give these probabilities only after an approximate rescaling, because the probability of a merged mechanism is not exactly proportional to p.

Every noise channel in the generated circuit has probability equal to p, so the circuit at any p is the stored circuit with each noise argument multiplied by p / 1e-3.

## Decision

We store the circuit text generated at p = 1e-3 as package data, with a provenance file that records the generator's repository, its commit and the exact call.
The detector error model is derived from it with Stim.
Its columns are the distinct pairs of a detector set and an observable set, in order of first appearance in the flattened model, so that they agree with the published indices.
The model at another p is derived from the circuit with every noise argument scaled by p / 1e-3.
The columns do not depend on p, since they depend only on which mechanisms exist, so only the priors change.

## Consequences

The probability of every mechanism is exact at every p, and no approximation of the priors has to be defended or bounded.
The Stim version is pinned, because the derivation of the model from the circuit is part of the fixture.
A circuit builder from another source replaces the stored text only when its circuit equals the stored one; that replacement is a new decision record that supersedes this one.
If a circuit appears whose noise arguments are not all fixed multiples of p, the scaling rule has to be restated.
